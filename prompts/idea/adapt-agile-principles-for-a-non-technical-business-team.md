# Adapt Agile Principles for a Non-Technical Business Team

Customizes an Agile implementation plan for non-technical business departments to improve workflow visibility and manage shifting priorities.

Variables: department_name, company_name, team_responsibilities

## Prompt

I am the manager of the {{department_name}} department at {{company_name}}. My team is responsible for {{team_responsibilities}}. We often struggle with shifting priorities, long project lead times, and a lack of visibility into each other's work. I want to adapt Agile principles (like Scrum or Kanban) to improve our team's workflow.

Act as an Agile coach who specializes in applying Agile to business teams (like marketing, HR, or finance). Your task is to create a plan for my team to adopt a more Agile way of working.

The plan should include:

1.  **Choosing a Framework:**
    *   Explain the key differences between Scrum and Kanban.
    *   Recommend which framework would be a better starting point for my team and justify your recommendation.
2.  **Implementing the Chosen Framework:**
    *   **If Kanban:** Explain how to set up a Kanban board (with columns like "Backlog," "To Do," "In Progress," "Done"), establish Work-in-Progress (WIP) limits, and manage the flow of work.
    *   **If Scrum:** Explain how to define roles (Product Owner, Scrum Master), run ceremonies (Sprint Planning, Daily Stand-ups, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective), and manage a backlog.
3.  **Agile for Business Teams - Key Adaptations:**
    *   How do we adapt concepts like "sprints" or "user stories" for non-technical work?
    *   Provide examples of how a marketing team could use Agile to manage campaigns or a finance team could use it to manage the month-end close process.
4.  **Getting Started:**
    *   Outline a 30-day plan for my team to get started with Agile. What are the first steps we should take?

**Output Format:**

Please generate a practical implementation plan in Markdown. The plan should be easy for a non-technical audience to understand and should provide concrete examples.

**Constraints:**

*   The plan must avoid overly technical Agile jargon.
*   The recommendations must be tailored to the specific context of a business team, not a software development team.
*   The tone should be supportive, practical, and focused on the benefits of agility (e.g., flexibility, transparency, and faster delivery of value).

## Tips

This prompt addresses a common challenge: how to bring the benefits of Agile to non-technical parts of an organization.
